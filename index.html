Scoot · Payments

Giving payments its own release lifecycle.

Payments originally shipped as a microfrontend embedded inside consuming applications. That made a payment change dependent on each platform picking up and deploying a new module version.

We changed the boundary. Payment responsibility moved behind an in-house Hosted Payment Page, so consuming applications hand off to a capability that can evolve independently.

The architectural point was simple: payment methods, FX and DCC should not require coordinated releases across every booking or servicing platform.

My role

Drove the application-side technical direction and cross-platform adoption, working across payment and consuming product teams to move from embedded ownership to a cleaner service boundary.

Scoot · Loyalty

Making KrisFlyer redemption feel native inside a low-cost airline journey.

Loyalty sounds like a front-end feature until it meets inventory, fare logic, ancillaries, fulfilment, refunds and operational behaviour. Award Chart, Spontaneous Escape and wider redemption capability required those systems to behave as one customer journey.

I like this class of problem because architecture and business rules cannot be separated. The customer should see one coherent journey even when multiple airline and loyalty systems sit underneath it.

Scoot · Platform modernisation

Reducing application fragmentation without pretending every layer belongs to one team.

More than seven legacy web applications were modernised toward a common Angular SPA architecture. The goal was not simply to replace older front-end technology. It was to reduce one-off application patterns, make upgrades repeatable and give squads a clearer engineering baseline.

The applications do not talk directly to the core airline systems. Product squads work through their application middleware and a shared middleware layer that provides reusable airline logic. That shared layer is owned outside my team, but it forms an important boundary across squads.

That matters because product teams do not each need to understand or re-implement every airline-system integration detail. Shared capabilities stay shared; application teams can focus on their customer journeys.

My role

Set and reinforced common application patterns across the estate, led migration toward Angular SPAs, and shaped how consuming squads integrate with shared middleware capabilities without duplicating underlying airline logic.

Scoot · Production engineering

Shipping is only half the job. The other half is operating what you shipped.

Airline customer journeys have a low tolerance for “mostly working.” Release quality depends on regression discipline, security remediation, production observability, rollback decisions and teams knowing who owns the issue when multiple systems are involved.

I treat reliability as part of product delivery, not an operations problem that begins after engineering declares a feature complete.

My role

Set delivery expectations across squads, including regression readiness, production verification, security findings and incident ownership — while keeping enough technical depth to challenge failure modes.

Applied AI

I am more interested in whether AI survives an evaluation than whether it demos well.

I use AI in software delivery and domain experiments, but I prefer a measurable baseline, a test set and deterministic controls over “the model looked smart in the meeting.”

In one airline-domain experiment, we measured an LLM/RAG approach against representative real-world cases, added business context, found where additional context reduced accuracy, and refined the surrounding rules rather than assuming the larger prompt was automatically better.

1 · Hypothesis
Define what the model should improve.
2 · Evaluation set
Test representative real-world cases.
3 · Baseline
Measure before adding more context or agents.
4 · Controls
Keep business rules deterministic where failure matters.
5 · Decision
Adopt only when the result is better, not just newer.

Scoot · Payments

Decoupling payment releases from every platform that used payments.

Payments originally shipped as a microfrontend module embedded inside each consuming platform. That created a practical problem: when the payment team released a change, every platform had to pick up and deploy the new module version before customers could benefit from it.

We pushed to move that responsibility out of the host applications and into an in-house Hosted Payment Page. The integration boundary became simpler: platforms hand off to payments, while the payment team can evolve payment methods, FX and DCC without coordinating an application upgrade across every consumer.

The important change was not just technical. It gave the payment capability its own release lifecycle and clearer ownership.

Scoot · Loyalty

Making KrisFlyer redemption work inside the Scoot journey.

Award Chart and Spontaneous Escape let KrisFlyer members redeem miles directly for Scoot flights and ancillaries. That sounds simple at the UI. Underneath it means aligning airline inventory, loyalty rules, booking flows and operational behaviour without turning the customer journey into a collection of special cases.

The work is a good example of what I enjoy: cross-system product problems where architecture and business rules are inseparable.

Scoot · Platform modernisation

Modernising applications without creating eight different “modern” stacks.

More than seven legacy web applications were moved toward reusable SPA and API-first patterns. The point was not simply to replace old technology. It was to make teams faster by reducing one-off architecture and giving squads patterns they could own.

I also combined separate web and mobile tracks into cross-platform full-stack squads. Fewer hand-offs, clearer accountability, and one team owning the customer journey end to end.

How I work

I became a manager. I did not stop caring how the system works.

These are the habits I try to protect as teams and platforms get larger.

Teams should own journeys, not tickets.

Splitting responsibility by frontend, mobile and backend creates queues. I prefer squads that can take a customer problem from design to production.

Reliability is product work.

Security findings, production behaviour and operational constraints are part of delivery, not chores to throw over the wall after the feature is finished.

New technology has to earn its place.

I am experimenting with AI-augmented software delivery, but I care about measured improvement in quality and velocity more than adopting something because it is fashionable.
